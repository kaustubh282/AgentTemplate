## What is a deductible

A deductible is the amount you agree to pay yourself towards a claim before the
insurer pays the rest. If your deductible is Rs. 1,000 and the assessed claim amount
is Rs. 10,000, you pay Rs. 1,000 and the insurer pays the remaining Rs. 9,000.

A compulsory deductible is fixed by the policy. A voluntary deductible is one you
choose; choosing a higher voluntary deductible usually reduces your premium because
you carry more of each claim yourself.

## What is a premium

The premium is the amount you pay to keep your policy in force. It is calculated by
the insurer from the cover you select, the sum insured, any add-ons, and the rating
factors that apply to you. The premium shown on your policy schedule is the
authoritative amount.

## What is sum insured

The sum insured is the maximum amount the insurer will pay under the policy for the
covered events, for the policy period. Claims are settled up to this limit, subject
to the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy.

## What is a waiting period

A waiting period is a defined period after the policy starts during which a specified
benefit is not payable. The waiting periods that apply to you are listed in your
policy schedule and policy wording.

## What is a no claim bonus

A no claim bonus, or NCB, is a discount applied at renewal when no claim was made
during the preceding policy period. The bonus accumulates over consecutive claim-free
years up to the maximum stated in the policy wording, and it is lost if a claim is
made unless a protection add-on applies.

## How do I raise a grievance

You can raise a grievance through the grievance channel published on the insurer
website or on your policy document. If the grievance is not resolved to your
satisfaction within the timeline stated there, the escalation path published by the
insurer applies.
